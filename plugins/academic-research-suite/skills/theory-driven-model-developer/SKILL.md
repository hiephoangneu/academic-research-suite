# Theory-Driven Research Model Developer

Produce an academically defensible, publication-oriented literature review REPORT, not a source catalogue or a decorative conceptual diagram. The final product must trace each proposed construct and hypothesis to an explanatory mechanism and properly qualified evidence. Respect the researcher's intellectual choices while independently challenging weak constructs, unsupported arrows, and claims of novelty.

## 1. Interpret the research input

1. Identify whether the user provides (A) a nascent idea, (B) a topic or research question, or (C) a preliminary model/diagram. Record the focal phenomenon, dependent construct(s), unit and level of analysis, population, setting, time horizon, theoretical objective, and intended study design when supplied.
2. For A/B, identify two or three credible conceptual formulations if the phenomenon is genuinely ambiguous, then choose and state the best working formulation unless an essential decision blocks the work. For C, reconstruct the user's model as a **provisional proposal**, retain a faithful baseline, and test it against competing mechanisms and evidence. Do not silently reclassify variables.
3. Differentiate individual, team, organisational, sectoral, and national constructs and data. Identify temporal order; distinguish perception, capability, practice, intention, adoption, and performance.
4. State assumptions and information gaps succinctly. Ask questions only when absolutely necessary to avoid producing the wrong research product.
5. Select an integrative/critical theory-building review by default. Do not label a web-search-based integrative review systematic, exhaustive, PRISMA-compliant, or a meta-analysis.

## 2. Conduct live web-based evidence discovery and verification

1. **Use web search** for all external literature claims. Search multiple independent concept blocks: focal phenomenon and synonyms; each core construct and variants; theory and mechanism; tested relationships; opposing, null, and conditional findings; and relevant time/context boundaries. Search both seminal literature and the latest available evidence, without assuming a fixed year range is universally appropriate.
2. Use publisher pages, DOI metadata, original article PDFs or accessible manuscripts, trusted scholarly indexes, and institutional repositories. Crossref/OpenAlex/Google Scholar may aid discovery or metadata lookup but discovery snippets are not sufficient support for nuanced findings. If accessible, use subscription databases only within the user's permissions and record database coverage.
3. Perform backward/forward citation chaining on pivotal studies when tools permit it. For each study actually used, check authors, year, title, outlet, DOI/stable URL, research design, measurement, context, principal result, and whether the result supports the precise claim.
4. Clearly tag full-text, abstract-only, metadata-only, or secondary-source access. An abstract may only support claims it actually states; inaccessible statistical detail is **not verified**. Never fabricate an author, DOI, finding, coefficient, sample, citation count, or access status. Do not invent sources to reach a target reference count.
5. Seek contradicting and null findings deliberately. Separate evidence of absence from absence of evidence, association from causal identification, and theoretical propositions from observed results.
6. Record date searched, representative exact queries, sources/platforms searched, coverage/selection limits, and verification status. If live web search is unavailable, do not pretend it occurred: explicitly offer a provisional theory/model outline and label the literature review incomplete.
7. Follow [references/evidence-verification.md](references/evidence-verification.md) for the search log, source ledger, inclusion discipline, and evidence quality rules.

## 3. Review theoretical foundations as a contest of explanations

1. Identify the main explanatory mechanism before selecting a popular theory. For each candidate theory, report its foundational reference (verified), original explanatory domain, constructs, assumptions, level of analysis, causal/temporal logic, boundary conditions, main critiques, and actual role in this study.
2. Compare at least two theoretically plausible lenses when the field admits competing explanations. Select a primary lens and only complementary theories that add a **distinct** mechanism; explain where assumptions align or conflict. Do not combine theories solely to increase the apparent theoretical depth.
3. Distinguish theory-derived arrows from plausible but novel extensions; state their status. Give critical treatment to alternative and reverse-causal explanations.
4. Articulate what the proposed synthesis changes in theoretical understanding, such as mechanism, boundary condition, competing explanation, sequence, nonlinearity, cross-level linkage, or configuration. Never assert a theory or linkage has never been studied without search evidence adequate to support that claim.
5. Follow [references/theory-to-model.md](references/theory-to-model.md) for mechanism mapping and competing-model tests.

## 4. Synthesize empirical findings

1. Organise the empirical review by substantive debates, constructs, relationships, mechanisms, and conditions, not paper-by-paper summaries.
2. Report study characteristics that materially qualify inferences: sample and setting, data type, timing, design, measurement, identification, estimator, key direction/significance when actually reported, uncertainty, and robustness.
3. Explain agreements and disagreements using theoretical mechanisms first; then examine measurement, context, sample, temporal ordering, publication bias, and research design. State whether a proposed relationship is strongly, weakly, inconsistently, or not yet supported.
4. Separate highly relevant direct evidence from adjacent-context analogies; label extrapolation. Do not portray non-significant findings as zero effects or infer causality from PLS-SEM, cross-sectional survey models, or observational regression alone.
5. Prepare an auditable evidence matrix that ties each major conclusion to verifiable sources and access level, including negative or opposing evidence.

## 5. Derive and prioritise research gaps

1. Identify gaps in substantive explanation, theory integration, mechanisms, contradictory findings, boundary conditions, construct differentiation, research design/identification, or level of analysis.
2. For every candidate gap, specify: established knowledge; exact unresolved tension; verified supporting sources and counterevidence; the theoretical stakes; what the proposed model changes; and what empirical design could actually test.
3. Reject novelty arguments based solely on a different country, industry, sample, data collection year, method, or an arbitrary new moderator. Context is theoretically meaningful only when it changes mechanisms or boundary conditions.
4. Rank gaps by theoretical consequence, evidentiary support, feasibility, and risk. Select one main research gap and optionally one to two supporting gaps. Distinguish a promising research opportunity from a verified absence in the literature.

## 6. Develop hypotheses and the construct-level model

1. Establish an explicit construct dictionary. For **each proposed latent construct**, state name/acronym, conceptual definition, theoretical grounding, unit/level, domain and distinctness, dimensions and candidate role (predictor, outcome, mediator, moderator, higher-order construct, control), and source provenance.
2. Explain operationalisation at the defensible level: candidate reflective/formative/composite interpretation, scope of any higher-order dimensions, established measurement scale source where verified, and conceptual adaptation risks. Never invent validated questionnaire items or present suggested items as validated scales. Declare uncertain measurement choices as pending empirical/content validation.
3. For each hypothesis, use the chain **theory -> mechanism -> relevant empirical evidence and counterevidence -> conditions -> testable directional/non-directional prediction**. Give stable identifiers H1, H2a etc.; include mediation/moderation/nonlinearity/configuration only when justified and measurable.
4. Explain proposed indirect effects with temporally coherent stages; a significant product term alone does not prove a causal mechanism. Make moderation direction and interacting levels clear. For nonlinear/threshold/configurational proposals, explain why a simple linear net-effect hypothesis is insufficient.
5. Map every arrow to one hypothesis and at least one verified theoretical/empirical justification (or clearly mark exploratory). Check construct overlap, duplicated paths, cross-level fallacies, omitted explanations, collider/bad controls, reverse direction, and testing feasibility.
6. Compare the recommended model with a lean baseline and at least one credible rival structure. Prefer parsimony and theoretical discriminating power to maximal hypothesis count.
7. Choose methods only to match the question and data (e.g., PLS-SEM, CB-SEM, panel, experiment, fsQCA); do not confuse statistical implementability with identification of causal effects. Outline data/design requirements without claiming collection or testing has occurred.
8. Follow [references/theory-to-model.md](references/theory-to-model.md) for hypothesis and construct audit templates.

## 7. Write the complete report and verify readiness

1. Deliver a **cohesive academic report** with narrative literature-review prose and the components in [references/report-template.md](references/report-template.md): scope/method and coverage, thematic empirical synthesis, critical theory review, prioritised gaps and contributions, systematic hypothesis arguments, construct dictionary, model (diagram or explicit arrow list), rival models, research design considerations, and verified APA 7 references.
2. Give both a readable main narrative and traceability matrices. Place citations near the statements they support. Prefer verified primary literature, include classics alongside current findings, and distinguish full text from abstract-supported claims.
3. For any user-preselected model, include **original vs retained/revised/rejected** construct/arrow decisions with specific reasoning.
4. If evidence coverage is insufficient to responsibly substantiate a complete literature review or a specific hypothesis, identify the unsupported section explicitly; provide the strongest honest partial report, further search targets, and conditional hypotheses rather than fabricated certainty.
5. Before completion, apply the ten-point quality gate in [references/report-template.md](references/report-template.md). Avoid em dashes; use disciplined, publication-ready language without overclaiming.

## Scope and handoffs

Use `literature-review` for open-ended literature discovery, screening plans, and evidence mapping when a fully authored model-development report is not requested. Use `q1-manuscript-writer` when the framework and citations are already settled and the task is to polish a manuscript section. Use `citation-integrity-auditor` for exhaustive reference verification of an existing document, and `pls-sem-fsqca-reviewer` for technical measurement or model-estimation audits. Do not present a literature review as equivalent to original empirical testing.
