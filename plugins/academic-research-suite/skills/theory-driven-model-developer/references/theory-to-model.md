# Theory-to-Model Development Protocol

## 1. Theory selection and theoretical novelty

For each candidate theory, complete:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Verified foundational source | Actual original work and subsequent extensions |
| Explanandum | What the theory originally seeks to explain |
| Mechanism | A specific process or incentive, not just a label |
| Level and time | Actor, group, firm, market, institution, and temporal ordering |
| Assumptions | Conditions needed for the predicted effect |
| Predictions and scope | What the theory implies and cannot imply |
| Competing account | Alternative theoretical mechanism or rival lens |
| Theoretical advancement | Which accepted explanation or boundary this model changes |

Use the fewest theories that cover distinct mechanisms without contradiction. Explicitly state when the theoretical novelty is proposed rather than established.

## 2. Construct dictionary and validity audit

Prepare a complete dictionary:

| Code | Formal construct and definition | Unit/level | Role and expected timing | Conceptual dimensions | Closely related construct and distinction | Verified scale/source or 'pending' | Measurement considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Never equate survey items to constructs without content validity. For reflective indicators, justify a common latent cause and indicator interchangeability where appropriate; for formative/composite indicators, justify domain coverage, distinct contribution, and conceptual direction. Higher-order constructs require explicit lower-order components and theoretical logic. Differentiate variables representing ability, adoption, usage, expectation, perception, and results.

If the user asks for candidate item wording, write **new, unvalidated candidate items**, separated from verified/adapted published scales and accompanied by an explicit validation requirement. Do not claim permission to reproduce copyrighted questionnaires.

## 3. Hypothesis argument card

Repeat for every proposed H#:

1. **Relationship:** construct X, construct Y, sign/form, unit, and time.
2. **Theory:** verified mechanism and assumptions. Why should X change or predict Y?
3. **Supporting evidence:** verified studies directly testing X-Y and adjacent mechanism evidence; note methodological quality.
4. **Counterevidence:** opposing, weak or null findings and plausible reasons for differences.
5. **Boundary and rivals:** contextual conditions, endogeneity, cross-level or reverse-causal alternatives.
6. **Formal hypothesis:** one falsifiable statement, calibrated to the available design.
7. **Traceability:** construct IDs, sources and corresponding model edge.

A plausible speculative arrow may be retained only if clearly labelled **exploratory** and if the main report honestly notes its limited evidence. Do not stack mediators and moderators for novelty alone.

## 4. Model-to-theory contribution mapping

| Proposed relation | Mechanism | Theory | Evidence status | Added theoretical insight | Feasible empirical test | Rival explanation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Compare at minimum:

- Baseline model: the smallest plausible direct relationship model.
- Preferred model: adds mechanisms or conditions required to answer the real theoretical gap.
- Rival model: plausible reverse, alternative mediator, overlapping construct, competing boundary, nonlinear form, or other substantively distinct structure.

If a preferred model cannot be meaningfully distinguished from baseline using realistic design and data, reduce the claims or simplify the model.

## 5. Study design guardrails

- **Survey/PLS-SEM or CB-SEM:** suitable for specified latent-variable structures when measurement and sample requirements are met; cross-sectional mediation is not proof of temporal causation, and model fit/predictive ability does not identify causal effects.
- **Panel methods:** discuss timing, fixed unobserved heterogeneity, reverse causality, dynamic bias, shocks, and identification assumptions; GMM is not a universal cure for endogeneity.
- **Experiments/quasi-experiments:** design and assumptions can support stronger causal estimands; still inspect external validity and measurement validity.
- **fsQCA/NCA:** use only when asymmetry, configurational sufficiency, or necessary conditions are theoretically integral. Do not substitute set-theoretic evidence for directional causal identification.
- **Mixed methods:** state each component's unique inferential contribution and how results will be integrated.

Recommend testable construct operationalisation and design requirements, not fictional results, fixed sample sizes without power analysis, or unverified fit thresholds.

## 6. Research gap stress test

A research gap fails if it is merely an unstudied setting without a contextual mechanism, a variable insertion without theoretical logic, a claimed novelty without credible search coverage, or an inference that the proposed data cannot meaningfully address. Identify which theoretical belief the study would revise, constrain, reconcile, or falsify if its predictions are supported or rejected.
