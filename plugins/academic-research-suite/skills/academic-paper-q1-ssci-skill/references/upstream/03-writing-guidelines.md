# Writing Guidelines for Each Section

---

## General Principles (Apply to ALL Sections)

### Sentence Construction
- **One idea per sentence.** Split compound ideas.
- **Front-load the main point.** Subject and verb should appear early.
- **Target length:** 15-25 words. Maximum: 35 words.
- **Vary rhythm:** Alternate short (emphasis) and medium sentences.

### Paragraph Construction
- **Topic sentence first** (declares the paragraph's point).
- **Evidence sentences** (2-5 sentences with citations/data).
- **Closing transition** (synthesizes or links forward).
- **Length:** 100-200 words (4-8 sentences). Never single-sentence paragraphs.

### Prohibited Constructions

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Replace with |
|---------|----------------|
| Em dash (—) | Comma, semicolon, colon, parentheses, or period |
| Double hyphen (--) | Same alternatives as above |
| Contractions (don't, can't) | Do not, cannot |
| "There is/are" (expletive) | Rephrase with strong subject |
| "It is important to note that" | State the point directly |
| "In order to" | "To" |
| "Due to the fact that" | "Because" |
| "A number of" | "Several" or state the exact number |
| "In terms of" | "Regarding" or rephrase |
| "At this point in time" | "Currently" |
| "Has the ability to" | "Can" |
| "It is worth mentioning" | State it directly |
| "Needless to say" | Remove entirely (if needless, why say it?) |

## Section-Specific Guidelines

### Introduction

**Goal:** Convince the reader that (1) the topic matters, (2) there is
a gap, and (3) your study fills it.

**Opening hook strategies:**
- Start with a striking statistic: "Over 2.7 billion people lack reliable
  internet access (ITU, 2025)."
- Start with a real-world problem: "Teacher attrition in rural schools
  continues to threaten educational equity across developing nations."
- Start with a trend: "The rapid integration of artificial intelligence
  into higher education has prompted urgent questions about pedagogical
  adaptation."

**Avoid:**
- Starting with a dictionary definition ("According to Merriam-Webster...")
- Starting with "Since the beginning of time..." or similar cliches
- Starting with "In recent years..." (overused; find a more specific hook)

**Gap statement patterns:**
- "Despite extensive research on X, the relationship between Y and Z
  remains underexplored."
- "While prior studies have examined X in [context A], limited research
  has investigated this phenomenon in [context B]."
- "However, existing studies have primarily relied on [method], leaving
  [aspect] unexamined."

**Purpose statement pattern:**
- "The purpose of this study is to [investigate/examine/explore] the
  [relationship/effect/experience] of [X] on [Y] among [population]
  in [context]."

### Literature Review

**Goal:** Demonstrate mastery of the field, build the theoretical case
for your study, and derive hypotheses logically.

**Organization:** ALWAYS thematic. Never chronological (listing studies
in order of publication year).

**Synthesis vs. summary:**
- ❌ Summary: "Smith (2023) found X. Jones (2024) found Y. Lee (2025) found Z."
- ✅ Synthesis: "Several studies have found X (Jones, 2024; Lee, 2025;
  Smith, 2023), although the magnitude of the effect varies by context."

**Critical engagement:**
- Compare findings across studies
- Identify methodological strengths and weaknesses
- Note contradictions and propose explanations
- Show how the literature points toward your study

### Methodology

**Goal:** Convince the reader that your methods are rigorous, appropriate,
and reproducible.

**Tone:** Precise, factual, detailed. No persuasion needed.

**Key requirements:**
- Every methodological choice needs justification
- "We used [method] because [reason]" or "Following [Author, Year], we..."
- Report ALL relevant numbers (sample size, response rate, Cronbach's alpha)
- Name software WITH version numbers

**Common weaknesses to avoid:**
- Insufficient sample size justification
- Missing validity/reliability evidence
- Vague procedure descriptions
- Overlooking ethical approval

### Results

**Goal:** Present findings objectively, organized by RQ or hypothesis.

**Rules:**
- NO interpretation in Results (save it for Discussion)
- Report exact statistics: *F*(2, 187) = 14.32, *p* < .001, eta-squared = .13
- Report effect sizes alongside significance tests
- Report confidence intervals when possible
- Use past tense: "The analysis revealed..."

**Table/figure guidelines:**
- Reference every table/figure in the text BEFORE it appears
- "As shown in Table 3, the regression model was significant..."
- Tables for exact numbers; figures for patterns and trends
- Never duplicate the same data in both a table and a figure

### Discussion

**Goal:** Interpret findings, compare with literature, and articulate
the "so what?"

**Structure for each finding:**
1. Restate the finding briefly
2. Compare with prior research (confirm, extend, or contradict)
3. Explain through the theoretical framework
4. State the implication

**Pattern:**
> "The finding that X significantly predicted Y (beta = .34, p < .001)
> is consistent with [Theory] and aligns with previous research
> (Author, Year; Author, Year). This suggests that [interpretation].
> Practically, this implies that [recommendation]."

**Limitations guidelines:**
- Be specific: "The cross-sectional design limits causal inferences"
  (not "this study has limitations")
- Pair each limitation with a future research suggestion
- Be honest but not self-destructive
- 3-5 specific limitations is standard

### Conclusion

**Goal:** Leave a lasting impression of the study's value.

**Do:**
- Restate purpose and main findings (2-3 sentences)
- Emphasize contributions (theoretical, practical)
- End with a forward-looking statement

**Do NOT:**
- Introduce new findings or data
- Add new citations
- Repeat the entire Discussion
- Use "In conclusion, this study found that..." as the first sentence
  (too mechanical; vary the opening)
