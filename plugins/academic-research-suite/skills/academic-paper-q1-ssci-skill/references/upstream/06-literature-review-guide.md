# Literature Review Guide for Q1 SSCI Papers

---

## Purpose of the Literature Review

A literature review in a Q1 SSCI paper is NOT a summary of what others
have found. It is a **critical, analytical synthesis** that:

1. Establishes the theoretical foundation for the study
2. Maps the current state of knowledge
3. Identifies gaps, contradictions, and opportunities
4. Builds a logical argument leading to research questions or hypotheses
5. Demonstrates the author's command of the field

---

## Step 1: Define the Search Strategy

### Database Selection
| Field | Primary Databases |
|-------|------------------|
| Education | ERIC, Web of Science, Scopus, PsycINFO |
| Management | Web of Science, Scopus, ABI/INFORM, EBSCO Business Source |
| Psychology | PsycINFO, PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus |
| Economics | EconLit, JSTOR, Web of Science, Scopus |
| Sociology | Sociological Abstracts, Web of Science, Scopus |

### Search Terms
- Develop a keyword matrix combining:
  - Construct 1 terms (synonyms, related terms)
  - Construct 2 terms
  - Context terms (population, geography)
- Use Boolean operators: AND, OR, NOT
- Apply truncation (*) for word variations

### Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria
| Criterion | Include | Exclude |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Date | 2020-2026 (core); seminal works any date | Pre-2015 unless seminal |
| Language | English | Non-English |
| Source type | Peer-reviewed journals | Conference papers, theses, blogs |
| Quality | Q1-Q2 journals preferred | Predatory journals |

## Step 2: Organize Thematically

### How to Identify Themes
1. Read abstracts and findings of all selected articles
2. Create a coding matrix (spreadsheet):
   - Columns: Author, Year, Purpose, Method, Key Findings, Theme
3. Group articles by common themes, concepts, or debates
4. Order themes logically (foundational to specific)

### Theme Structure Template
```
THEME: [Theme Name]

Definition/introduction (1-2 sentences)
├── Sub-theme A: Finding pattern across studies
│   ├── Supporting evidence (Author1, Year; Author2, Year)
│   └── Contradictory evidence (Author3, Year)
├── Sub-theme B: Another pattern
│   └── Supporting evidence
└── Connection to current study (1-2 sentences)
```

---

## Step 3: Synthesize, Do Not Summarize

### Summary (AVOID):
> Smith (2023) studied the effect of X on Y and found a positive
> relationship. Jones (2024) also examined X and Y and found similar
> results. Lee (2025) studied X in a different context and found
> mixed results.

### Synthesis (TARGET):
> A consistent positive relationship between X and Y has been documented
> across multiple contexts (Jones, 2024; Smith, 2023), with effect sizes
> ranging from small to moderate. However, when examined in [specific
> context], the relationship appears contingent on [moderating variable]
> (Lee, 2025), suggesting that contextual factors play a significant
> role in shaping this relationship. This inconsistency warrants further
> investigation, particularly in [understudied context].

---

## Step 4: Build Toward the Gap

### Gap Identification Strategies

| Gap Type | Signal Phrase |
|----------|-------------|
| Population gap | "Most studies have focused on [population A]; few have examined [population B]." |
| Context gap | "While X has been investigated in [context A], limited research exists in [context B]." |
| Methodological gap | "Existing studies have predominantly relied on [method A], leaving [aspect] unexamined through [method B]." |
| Theoretical gap | "No study has examined X through the lens of [theory]." |
| Contradictory findings | "The inconsistent findings regarding X suggest the need for further investigation considering [factor]." |
| Temporal gap | "Given the rapid changes in [field], updated empirical evidence is needed." |

---

## Step 5: Derive Hypotheses (For Quantitative Studies)

### Hypothesis Development Pattern

```
1. Present the theoretical prediction
   "According to [Theory], [construct A] should [relationship] [construct B]
    because [mechanism]."

2. Present the empirical evidence
   "Consistent with this prediction, [Author, Year] found [result].
    Similarly, [Author, Year] reported [result]."

3. State the hypothesis
   "Based on the theoretical rationale and empirical evidence, we
    hypothesize that:
    H1: [Construct A] positively relates to [Construct B]."
```

## Common Reviewer Complaints About Literature Reviews

| Complaint | How to Avoid |
|-----------|-------------|
| "The review is too descriptive" | Synthesize across studies; identify patterns |
| "Key references are missing" | Search multiple databases; include seminal works |
| "The review is outdated" | Ensure 40%+ from the last 5 years |
| "The gap is not clear" | State the gap explicitly in 1-2 dedicated paragraphs |
| "The review does not connect to the study" | End each theme with a link to your RQ/hypothesis |
| "The theoretical framework is weak" | Dedicate a full subsection; justify the choice |
