# Sample Introduction (Annotated)

> **Topic:** The impact of formative assessment on self-regulated learning
> in higher education.
> **Target journal:** Studies in Higher Education (Q1 SSCI)

---

## [HOOK: Opening with a striking problem statement]

The ability to regulate one's own learning has been recognized as one
of the most critical determinants of academic success in higher education
(Zimmerman, 2013). Yet, a significant proportion of university students
enter their programs without adequate self-regulation skills, leading to
high attrition rates, poor academic performance, and diminished
well-being (Broadbent and Poon, 2015). As institutions increasingly
adopt student-centered pedagogies, understanding the instructional
mechanisms that foster self-regulated learning (SRL) has become an urgent
priority for educators and researchers alike.

> **[NOTE: Opens with an established claim (Zimmerman), immediately
> connects to a real problem (attrition, poor performance), and ends
> with why this matters NOW. No em dashes used.]**

## [BACKGROUND: Defining key constructs]

Self-regulated learning refers to the process through which learners
actively set goals, monitor their progress, and adjust strategies to
achieve desired outcomes (Pintrich, 2000). Within this framework,
three cyclical phases have been identified: forethought (planning and
goal setting), performance (strategy execution and self-monitoring),
and self-reflection (evaluation and adaptation) (Zimmerman, 2002).
Formative assessment, defined as the ongoing process of gathering and
using evidence of student learning to inform instruction and provide
feedback (Black and Wiliam, 1998), has been proposed as a powerful
instructional mechanism for supporting each of these phases.

> **[NOTE: Key constructs (SRL and formative assessment) are defined
> with seminal citations. Each definition is precise and concise.]**

## [LITERATURE SYNTHESIS: Thematic summary]

A growing body of empirical evidence supports the relationship between
formative assessment practices and SRL outcomes. Studies employing survey
designs have consistently documented positive associations between
feedback quality and self-monitoring behaviors (Nicol and
Macfarlane-Dick, 2006; Panadero and Jonsson, 2013). Furthermore,
experimental research has demonstrated that structured self-assessment
activities enhance students' metacognitive awareness and goal-setting
accuracy (Andrade, 2019; Yan and Brown, 2017). In the context of
higher education specifically, Winstone et al. (2017) found that
students who actively engaged with formative feedback demonstrated
significantly higher academic performance compared to those who
received feedback passively.

However, the evidence is not entirely consistent. Several studies have
reported that the effectiveness of formative assessment depends heavily
on contextual factors, including the clarity of assessment criteria
(Sadler, 2010), the timeliness of feedback (Hattie and Timperley, 2007),
and the degree of student agency in the assessment process (Boud and
Molloy, 2013). These findings suggest that the formative
assessment-SRL relationship may be more nuanced than previously assumed.

> **[NOTE: This section synthesizes across studies (not just summarizes
> them individually). It identifies both consistent findings and
> contradictions, building toward the gap.]**

## [GAP: Explicitly stating what is missing]

Despite the growing interest in formative assessment and SRL, two
significant gaps remain in the literature. First, the majority of
existing studies have examined the effects of individual formative
assessment components (e.g., feedback alone, self-assessment alone) in
isolation, without investigating how multiple components interact to
influence SRL. Second, much of the research has been conducted in
Western educational contexts, and the generalizability of these
findings to higher education settings in Southeast Asia, where
pedagogical traditions and student expectations differ substantially,
has not been empirically tested (Nguyen and Walker, 2016).

> **[NOTE: Two specific, well-defined gaps. Each gap is justified
> with evidence. No vague "more research is needed" statements.]**

## [PURPOSE: Clear statement of aims and research questions]

The purpose of this study is to investigate the combined effects of
three formative assessment practices (instructor feedback, peer
assessment, and self-assessment) on self-regulated learning among
undergraduate students in Vietnamese universities. Specifically, this
study addresses the following research questions:

**RQ1:** To what extent do formative assessment practices (instructor
feedback, peer assessment, and self-assessment) predict self-regulated
learning among Vietnamese undergraduate students?

**RQ2:** Which formative assessment practice is the strongest predictor
of self-regulated learning?

**RQ3:** How do students perceive the role of formative assessment in
their self-regulation processes?

> **[NOTE: Purpose is specific (variables, population, context). Three
> RQs are numbered, specific, and directly linked to the gaps. RQ3
> signals a mixed-methods component.]**

## [SIGNIFICANCE: Why this study matters]

This study contributes to the literature in three ways. Theoretically,
it extends self-determination theory by examining how formative
assessment practices support the three basic psychological needs
(autonomy, competence, and relatedness) that underpin self-regulated
learning. Methodologically, it employs a mixed-methods design that
combines survey data with semi-structured interviews, providing both
breadth and depth of understanding. Practically, the findings offer
evidence-based recommendations for faculty development programs seeking
to integrate formative assessment into higher education curricula in
Southeast Asian contexts.

> **[NOTE: Three clear contributions (theoretical, methodological,
> practical). Each is specific, not generic.]**
