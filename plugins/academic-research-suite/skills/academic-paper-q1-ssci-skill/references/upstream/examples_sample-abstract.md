## The Abstract

**[BACKGROUND]** Self-regulated learning (SRL) is a critical predictor
of academic success in higher education, yet many students lack the
skills to regulate their learning effectively. Formative assessment has
been proposed as a mechanism for developing SRL; however, empirical
evidence on its combined effects remains limited, particularly in
non-Western contexts.

**[PURPOSE]** This mixed-methods study examined the effects of three
formative assessment practices (instructor feedback, peer assessment,
and self-assessment) on self-regulated learning among undergraduate
students in Vietnam.

**[METHOD]** Survey data were collected from 312 students across three
universities using the Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire
(MSLQ). Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 18 purposefully
selected participants. Hierarchical regression and thematic analysis
were employed for data analysis.

**[FINDINGS]** Instructor feedback (beta = .34, *p* < .001) and
self-assessment (beta = .28, *p* < .01) significantly predicted SRL,
while peer assessment did not reach significance. Qualitative findings
revealed that students valued timely, specific feedback but found peer
assessment stressful without adequate training.

**[IMPLICATIONS]** These findings advance self-determination theory and
suggest that faculty development programs should prioritize instructor
feedback quality and self-assessment scaffolding over peer assessment
quantity.
